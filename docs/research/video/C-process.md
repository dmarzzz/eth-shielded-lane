# What a launch video must satisfy for a public US company in regulated finance

Scope: an official launch video (30 to 60 s hero, plus cut-downs) for a NYSE-listed company operating in regulated finance, using Circle (NYSE: CRCL) and Coinbase (NASDAQ: COIN) as the reference cases. "Looks good" is assumed. This covers everything else the video must clear before it ships. Researched 2026-09-18. Every claim is tied to a URL; where the source is a secondary summary rather than the primary rule, that is noted.

Applicability note: not every rule below binds every company. FINRA Rule 2210 binds broker-dealers (Coinbase has BD affiliates; Circle's stablecoin issuance is not a BD activity). NYDFS 23 NYCRR 200 binds BitLicensees and NY limited purpose trust companies. GENIUS Act marketing prohibitions bind payment stablecoin issuers and "other parties." Reg FD and the PSLRA bind every SEC registrant. Treat the list as a superset and have counsel strike what does not apply.

## 1. Brand system compliance: how public companies govern motion

Large companies treat motion as a governed brand element with the same status as logo, type and color. Three public examples with a motion section:

**IBM Carbon Design System (motion).** Carbon defines two motion styles, "productive" ("creates a sense of efficiency and responsiveness, while remain subtle and out of the way") and "expressive" ("delivers enthusiastic, vibrant, and highly visible movement"), with named easing curves and duration tokens. Standard easing: productive `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0.38, 0.9)`, expressive `cubic-bezier(0.4, 0.14, 0.3, 1)`; entrance and exit curves are specified separately. Duration tokens run `fast-01` 70 ms through `slow-02` 700 ms. Explicit prohibitions: easing that is "unnatural, distracting, or purely decorative" and easing suggesting "bounce, stretch, or sudden stops." Source: https://v10.carbondesignsystem.com/guidelines/motion/overview/ and https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/motion/overview/

**Uber (motion guidelines, APAC brand video system).** Uber's motion guidelines specify direction ("Animation is always moving from left to right, giving a sense of progress"), speed ("loosely based on the animation that is found within the app"), a wordmark animation cut to fit 6 s YouTube ads, a preference for the "logobit" over the wordmark as a video overlay for legibility, a rule that "supers for video should be short and concise," and a single motion style held constant across 15 countries and 11 languages. Clear space is defined as at least the height of the "U" in the wordmark. Sources: https://www.behance.net/gallery/69157695/Uber-Motion-Guidelines and https://frictionlesshq.com/brand-guide/uber/ (secondary summary of the clear-space rule). Uber's brand system lists motion as one of nine governed elements alongside logo, composition, typography, iconography, color, photography, illustration and tone of voice: https://designers-choice.net/uber-brand-guidelines/ (secondary).

**Coinbase (2022 identity by Moniker).** The rebrand shipped with "logos, color palette, layouts, motion, and comprehensive set of brand guidelines." The motion principle is "constant but intentional movement" tied to the idea that "the cryptoeconomy is always open," with a context split: "In product, the motion is subtle and intentionally used to convey crucial information clearly, in marketing, the motion flexes to become more engaging and enticing." Sources: https://monikersf.com/project/coinbase/ and https://www.itsnicethat.com/news/moniker-coinbase-rebrand-graphic-design-250322. Coinbase's open-sourced design system (CDS) publishes the LogoMark component and clear-space rule (minimum clear space equal to the height of the "C" in the wordmark): https://cds.coinbase.com/components/media/LogoMark/ and https://www.coinbase.com/blog/Coinbase-has-open-sourced-its-design-system

**Circle (2023 refresh by Brand New School with Circle Creative Lab).** Circle's VP Creative Cameron Ewing documents the system: an abstract 3D illustration system (object library of portals, paths, stairs), a materials and color library, a graphic system for photography plus 3D plus typographic grid, a social media toolkit, a data-visualization language with infographic templates, and a style guide "for scaling across platforms." Theme is "fluidity." Credits: Jonathan Notaro (CCO), Chloë Greene and Jeff Welk (CDs), Jean-Michel Verbeeck (lead 3D) at Brand New School; Andy Hekimian and Kristine MacAulay (art direction) and Hannah Post (brand strategy) at Circle. Sources: https://cameronewing.substack.com/p/refreshing-the-circle-brand-system, https://www.brandnewschool.com/work/circle-illustrated-branding-system, https://www.circle.com/blog/new-breakthroughs-new-momentum-now-circle-has-a-new-look. The USDC mark was separately refined by Mother Design (parentheses-encircled form, customized Monument Grotesk wordmark): https://lbbonline.com/news/Mother-Design-Brand-Identity-Circles-USDC-Stablecoin (page returned 403 on fetch; summary from search index).

**Circle's public brand rules (pressroom).** Circle logo: "clearspace is 2x" on all sides, 120 px minimum for web. USDC and EURC: minimum 24 px height in digital. Arc: minimum clear space "defined by the height of the inner arch (denoted as 1x)." Brand use policy PDF: "Do not alter, stretch, or modify the assets." Logos are supplied as zips with approved light and dark variants for Circle, USDC, EURC, USYC, Arc and cirBTC. Source: https://www.circle.com/pressroom

**What "brand motion language" means in practice (synthesis of the above).** A governed motion system fixes: (a) named easing curves and duration tiers; (b) a direction or physics metaphor; (c) a logo reveal or "sting" at fixed durations (6 s and 15 s cuts need their own logo timing); (d) a legibility rule for supers over video; (e) type, color and clear-space rules inherited from the static system; (f) a product-vs-marketing intensity split. A launch video must be reviewable against these, which means the studio needs the guidelines, the logo animation source files, the approved typeface with a video-capable license (see section 2), and the color tokens in the working color space (BT.709 for SDR delivery per YouTube: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1722171?hl=en).

## 2. Legal and comms review for a public company

### 2a. Regulation FD and forward-looking statements

**The rule.** 17 CFR 243.100: when an issuer or "person acting on its behalf" discloses material nonpublic information to covered persons, it must make public disclosure simultaneously (intentional) or promptly (non-intentional). Source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-243/section-243.100. "Public disclosure" under 243.101(e) means furnishing or filing a Form 8-K, or using "another method (or combination of methods) of disclosure that is reasonably designed to provide broad, non-exclusionary distribution of the information to the public." Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/243.101

**Why it matters for a video.** If the launch video contains a metric or fact that is material and not yet public (circulation numbers, partner names, revenue-relevant volumes, launch dates), the video cannot be the first place it appears unless the channel itself qualifies. The SEC's 2013 Netflix report says social media can satisfy Reg FD only if "investors have been alerted about which social media will be used to disseminate such information," and that disclosure via a personal account "without advance notice to investors that the site may be used for this purpose" falls short. Source: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2013-2013-51htm and the underlying 21(a) report https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/investreport/34-69279.pdf. The 2008 interpretive release (34-58288) applies the same "recognized channel of distribution" test to company websites: https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/interp/2008/34-58288.pdf

**How Circle does it.** Circle furnishes launch press releases under Item 7.01 (Regulation FD Disclosure) as Exhibit 99.1 to Form 8-K, for example the September 2026 share purchase agreement release: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1876042/000187604226000267/crcl-20260904.htm, and ARC token presale 8-Ks: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1876042/000187604226000148/crcl-20260511.htm and https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1876042/000187604226000205/crcl-20260629.htm. The Arc mainnet launch (2026-09-16) went out as a Business Wire release plus a livestream at 2 PM ET on @arc on X, with the video following the release rather than preceding it: https://www.circle.com/pressroom/circle-launches-arc-mainnet-an-economic-operating-system-for-the-internet and https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260916108863/en/Circle-Launches-Arc-Mainnet-an-Economic-Operating-System-for-the-Internet. Practical rule: the wire release (and 8-K if IR deems it material) posts first; the video posts at or after that timestamp; every number in the video already exists in the release.

**Forward-looking statements.** The PSLRA safe harbor (15 U.S.C. 78u-5) protects a forward-looking statement that is "identified as a forward-looking statement, and is accompanied by meaningful cautionary statements identifying important factors that could cause actual results to differ materially" (78u-5(c)(1)(A)(i)). For oral statements (a VO or on-camera executive counts), the statement must be accompanied by a cautionary statement that actual results might differ materially and a reference to a readily available written document containing the risk factors (78u-5(c)(2)). Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/78u-5. Circle's Arc release carries the standard legend: "This communication contains forward-looking statements within the meaning of the Private Securities Litigation Reform Act of 1995. All statements other than statements of historical fact are forward-looking statements," and points to the Form 10-K risk factors (https://www.circle.com/pressroom/circle-launches-arc-mainnet-an-economic-operating-system-for-the-internet). Practice guidance on legends and when they are needed: https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2024/09/forward-looking-statements-safe-harbors-comp and https://www.thecorporatecounsel.net/blog/2024/09/forward-looking-statements-how-to-use-legends-plus-when-where-why.html

Implication for the script: words like "will," "soon," "the future of," "trillions," "every bank" turn a line into a forward-looking statement. Either cut them, rewrite as present-tense fact, or carry an end-card legend and a link to the release. Aspirational tag lines are usually tolerated as puffery; projected metrics and timelines are not.

### 2b. Financial product disclaimers

**FINRA Rule 2210 (broker-dealers).** Content standards in 2210(d)(1): communications "must be based on principles of fair dealing and good faith" and "be fair and balanced"; no "false, exaggerated, unwarranted, promissory or misleading" statements; no performance predictions or projections outside narrow exceptions. A retail communication (distributed to more than 25 retail investors in 30 days; video counts) must be approved by "an appropriately qualified registered principal of the member" before first use (2210(b)(1)(A)), and records must be kept including the approving principal's name and date (2210(b)(4)). Source: https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/2210

**FINRA crypto sweep findings (what fails).** FINRA's targeted exam found potential 2210 violations in about 70% of the 500+ crypto retail communications it reviewed. Practices flagged: "False statements or implications that Crypto Assets functioned like cash or cash equivalent instruments"; comparisons to stock or cash "without providing a sound basis"; "Unclear and misleading explanations of how Crypto Assets work and their core features and risks"; omitting how assets "are issued, held, transferred, or sold"; "Misrepresenting that the protections of the federal securities laws or FINRA rules applied"; misleading SIPC statements; and failure to differentiate between the member's own products and an affiliate's crypto products. Source: https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/guidance/sweep-letters-and-updates/update-crypto-asset-communications and https://www.finra.org/media-center/newsreleases/2024/finra-publishes-crypto-asset-communications-sweep-update. For a stablecoin video the "functions like cash" finding is the trap: "digital dollar" phrasing needs the issuer's standard qualifiers.

**FDIC Part 328 Subpart B (binds non-banks too).** 12 CFR 328.102 prohibits using the FDIC name or logo to represent that "any Uninsured Financial Product is insured or guaranteed by the FDIC"; a non-bank making any deposit insurance statement must "clearly and conspicuously disclose that the person is not an FDIC-insured depository institution"; non-deposit products presented near deposits must state they "are not insured by the FDIC; are not deposits; and may lose value"; a non-bank describing pass-through insurance must "clearly and conspicuously identify the insured depository institution(s)." Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/328.102. The 2024 final rule and its Q&A extend "non-deposit product" to crypto-assets explicitly and confirm Subpart B can apply to non-banks such as crypto companies: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/01/18/2023-28629/fdic-official-signs-and-advertising-requirements-false-advertising-misrepresentation-of-insured and https://www.fdic.gov/deposit-insurance/questions-and-answers-related-fdics-part-328-final-rule. FDIC's 2022 advisory to crypto firms: https://www.fdic.gov/news/financial-institution-letters/2022/fil22035b.pdf (PDF; text not extractable via fetch, cited for the record).

**GENIUS Act (payment stablecoin issuers).** Per Gibson Dunn's section-by-section summary: Section 4(a)(7) prohibits "representing that payment stablecoins are backed by the full faith and credit of the United States, guaranteed by the United States government, or subject to Federal deposit insurance or Federal share insurance," and prohibits "marketing a product in the United States as a payment stablecoin unless the product is issued pursuant to the Act"; Section 4(a)(6) bars "using any terms relating to the United States government in the name of a payment stablecoin or marketing payment stablecoins in a way that would cause a reasonable person to believe that the payment stablecoins are legal tender, issued by the United States, or guaranteed or approved by the United States government" (currency abbreviations such as USD are excepted). Up to $500,000 per violation. Effective on the earlier of 18 months after enactment (2027-01-18) or 120 days after final regulations. Source: https://www.gibsondunn.com/the-genius-act-a-new-era-of-stablecoin-regulation/ (congress.gov text returned 403 on fetch). FDIC's April 2026 proposed rule restates the prohibition and says disclaimers "may be components of complying": https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/10/2026-06974/genius-act-requirements-and-standards-for-fdic-supervised-permitted-payment-stablecoin-issuers-and

Visual implication: no dollar-bill imagery that reads as US currency, no eagles, seals, flags or Treasury-adjacent iconography around the stablecoin; no "legal tender" language; no "as safe as a bank account."

**NYDFS (BitLicensees and NY trust companies).** 23 NYCRR 200.18(a): a licensee may not advertise "in New York or to New York Residents without including the name of the Licensee and the legend that such Licensee is 'Licensed to engage in Virtual Currency Business Activity by the New York State Department of Financial Services.'" 200.18(b): all advertising and marketing materials, including "radio and television advertising," must be retained for at least seven years, with "audio and video scripts" kept. 200.18(c): no "false, misleading, or deceptive representations or omissions." Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/23-NYCRR-200.18. The June 2022 stablecoin guidance requires "clear, conspicuous redemption policies," redemption at par net of "ordinary, well-disclosed fees," and public monthly CPA attestations: https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry_guidance/industry_letters/il20220608_issuance_stablecoins. Circle's Arc release shows the pattern of a product-level regulatory disclaimer: "Arc has not been reviewed or approved by the New York State Department of Financial Services or any other regulatory authority" (https://www.circle.com/pressroom/circle-launches-arc-mainnet-an-economic-operating-system-for-the-internet).

**FTC substantiation and on-screen disclosures.** The FTC's Advertising Substantiation policy: objective claims "represent explicitly or by implication that the advertiser has a reasonable basis supporting these claims" before dissemination; lacking one is a Section 5 violation. Source: https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/ftc-policy-statement-regarding-advertising-substantiation. The .com Disclosures guide (2013): disclosures must be "clear and conspicuous regardless of the device or platform"; audio claims need audio disclosures, visual claims need visual disclosures displayed for a sufficient duration. Source: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2013/03/ftc-staff-revises-online-advertising-disclosure-guidelines and practitioner summary https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2013/03/ftcs-updated-com-disclosures-guidance. Concretely: every number on screen ("$74B in circulation," "100+ builders," "98.8% of agent volume") needs a dated source in the claims file, and a footnote or end card that a viewer can actually read at the cut-down's speed.

### 2c. Music and font licensing

**Music.** Any pre-existing track needs two licenses: a synchronization license from the composition owner (publisher) and a master use license from the recording owner (label). Both are negotiated separately. A usable license for a launch video specifies track, project, platforms/media, commercial use, territory and term. Sources: https://www.musicbed.com/articles/resources/types-of-music-licenses/ and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synchronization_rights. For a public-company launch, the standard ask is worldwide, all media including paid social and broadcast, in perpetuity, with cut-down and re-edit rights; anything narrower creates a takedown risk on a permanent brand asset. Keep the license PDF in the claims file.

**Fonts.** Monotype lists license classes by use: desktop ("print or static image formats"), web, digital ad (email and HTML5 ads), app, embedded/OEM, server, ePub. Source: https://www.monotype.com/font-licensing-explained-designers-and-brands. Foundry EULAs commonly restrict "titles for film/video, motion graphics and animated artwork" to a separate broadcast or motion tier; a desktop license may cover ordinary social video but not broadcast or high-production advertising: https://everpop.app/blog/can-you-use-any-font-in-your-videos and https://displaay.net/help/licenses. Custom corporate typefaces (Coinbase Sans, Circle's customized Monument Grotesk) are owned or licensed by the company; confirm the studio's use is covered by the company's license, not the studio's.

### 2d. Third-party marks (Ethereum, Zcash and similar)

**Ethereum.** ethereum.org's assets page is a download repository with no license or usage statement on the page itself: https://ethereum.org/en/assets/. The Terms of Use claim the Foundation name, "Ethereum Switzerland," "Ethereum Asia," the Foundation logo and related marks and state "You must not use such marks without the prior written permission of the Foundation," with no nominative-use carve-out: https://ethereum.org/en/terms-of-use/. ETHEREUM is a registered US trademark of Stiftung Ethereum (Reg. 5110579): https://trademarks.justia.com/866/34/ethereum-86634529.html. Community requests for clearer branding rights are unresolved: https://github.com/ethereum/ethereum-org-website/issues/11752. Practical route: descriptive, nominative use ("USDC on Ethereum") in text is defensible; using the diamond logo as a hero element is a permission question to brand@ethereum.org (contact listed at http://ethereum.github.io/ethereum-org/dist/brand).

**Zcash.** The Zcash Foundation owns the word mark and logos. Permitted without permission: truthful descriptions of relationship ("software for the Zcash cryptocurrency," not "Zcash software"), "We accept payment in Zcash," showing support if your own branding is more prominent and the logo links to z.cash. Prohibited: using the marks in your own trademark, domain or company name; implying sponsorship or endorsement; suggesting your site is an official resource. Requests go to trademark@zfnd.org. Source: https://zfnd.org/zcash-trademark-policy/ and https://github.com/zcash/logos. Ownership history: https://electriccoin.co/blog/electric-coin-co-donates-zcash-trademark-to-zcash-foundation/

**General rule.** Every third-party logo in the video gets a row in the rights log: mark owner, policy URL, permitted-use basis or written permission, and the exact frame range. Partner logos (Visa, Mastercard, BlackRock and the other Arc validators) need each partner's comms approval of the specific frame, not just of the press release.

## 3. Accessibility

**WCAG 2.2 success criteria that apply to a marketing video** (text from https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/):

- 1.2.2 Captions (Prerecorded), Level A: "Captions are provided for all prerecorded audio content in synchronized media."
- 1.2.3 Audio Description or Media Alternative (Prerecorded), Level A: a transcript-style alternative or audio description for prerecorded video.
- 1.2.5 Audio Description (Prerecorded), Level AA: "Audio description is provided for all prerecorded video content in synchronized media." For a video that is mostly typography and VO, an extended transcript plus a script where the VO already names what is on screen is the pragmatic path; a separate AD track is the strict path.
- 1.4.2 Audio Control, Level A: audio that plays automatically for more than 3 s must be pausable or independently mutable (matters for the website hero embed).
- 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum), Level AA: 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for large text; "Text that is part of a logo or brand name has no contrast requirement." Burned-in supers and captions are text.
- 1.4.11 Non-text Contrast, Level AA: 3:1 for UI components and meaningful graphics.
- 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide, Level A: auto-starting motion lasting more than 5 s must be pausable (website hero autoplay).
- 2.3.1 Three Flashes or Below Threshold, Level A: "Web pages do not contain anything that flashes more than three times in any one second period, or the flash is below the general flash and red flash thresholds."
- 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions, Level AAA: interaction-triggered motion can be disabled (respect `prefers-reduced-motion` on the hero).

**Flash threshold detail.** A general flash is "a pair of opposing changes in relative luminance of 10% or more of the maximum relative luminance (1.0) where the relative luminance of the darker image is below 0.80"; content passes with no more than three general or red flashes in any one second, or if concurrent flashes occupy no more than 25% of any 10 degree visual field (a 341 x 256 px rectangle at 1024 x 768 is the estimate). Red flash: a transition to or from saturated red where R/(R+G+B) >= 0.8 and the chromaticity difference exceeds 0.2. Source: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html. Tools: PEAT (Trace Center, free for web content, uses an earlier HardingFPA engine; its license prohibits use on material produced for broadcast, film or gaming): https://trace.umd.edu/peat/; HardingFPA for broadcast: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harding_test; W3C technique G15: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G15. Ofcom's guidance note is the broadcast reference and the ASA points advertisers to its Annex 1: https://www.ofcom.org.uk/__data/assets/pdf_file/0021/16248/gn_flash.pdf and https://www.asa.org.uk/news/flashing-images-in-advertising.html. A 3D-heavy brand system with strobing light passes or fast white flashes at cuts is where launch videos typically fail this.

**Caption quality norms.** FCC's 2014 quality standards require captions to be accurate, synchronous, complete and properly placed: https://www.3playmedia.com/blog/fccs-new-quality-standards-closed-captioning-video-programming/ (summary of the FCC ruling) and https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/closed-captioning-television. DCMP Captioning Key: 32-character lines, presentation rate of about 150 to 160 wpm for adult content: https://dcmp.org/learn/5-captioning-guidelines-for-the-dcmp. Platform norms: YouTube accepts SRT, VTT, TTML, DFXP, SCC and others (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2734698?hl=en, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2734796?hl=en); LinkedIn accepts SRT and autoplays muted (https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a424737, https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1327025); Meta lists captions as "Optional, but recommended" (https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide/update/video). YouTube supports audio description as an additional audio track for channels with multi-language audio: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16166822?hl=en and https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13338784?hl=en. Auto-generated captions do not meet the FCC accuracy bar; ship a human-checked SRT.

**Why "silent" matters.** Publishers reported about 85% of Facebook video views happened with sound off (Digiday, 2016-05-17): https://digiday.com/media/silent-world-facebook-video/. LinkedIn autoplays muted by default (https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a565326). The number is old and platform-specific, but the design rule it produced (the video must carry its argument with sound off) is now standard.

## 4. Localization and versioning

**Aspect ratios and where they go (platform specs).**

- X ads: 16:9, 1:1 or 9:16; 1920x1080, 1200x1200 (secondary sources say 1080x1080 for organic), 1080x1920; MP4/MOV, H.264 + AAC-LC, 30 fps; recommended 15 s or less, max 2:20 in ads: https://business.x.com/en/help/campaign-setup/creative-ad-specifications (page paywalled on fetch; specs from https://alladspecs.com/specs/x-twitter/video-ad and https://www.sendible.com/insights/social-media-video-specs). Organic: 140 s and 512 MB for non-Premium; Premium up to 4 hours at 1080p: https://help.x.com/en/using-x/premium-longer-videos
- LinkedIn video ads: 3 s to 30 min; 75 KB to 500 MB; 4:5 (1080x1350), 9:16 (1080x1920), 16:9 (1920x1080), 1:1 (up to 1920x1920); 30 fps recommended; captions optional via SRT: https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/ads/sponsored-content/video-ads/specs
- YouTube ads: bumper 5 to 6 s; non-skippable in-stream 15 to 60 s by subtype (secondary sources: 7 to 15 s standard, 16 to 30 s for CTV); skippable in-stream no max (under 3 min recommended); Shorts under 60 s; Masthead over 10 s recommended: https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2375464?hl=en and https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/11462260?hl=en. Upload encoding: MP4, H.264 High, AAC-LC 48 kHz 384 kbps stereo, 8 Mbps at 1080p24-30 SDR, 12 Mbps at 1080p48-60, 35 to 45 Mbps at 4K, BT.709: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1722171?hl=en. Thumbnails 1280x720, under 2 MB: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72431?hl=en
- Meta: MP4/MOV, H.264, AAC 128 kbps+; Feed 4:5 at 1440x1800; Stories and Reels 9:16 at 1080x1920; 1 s to 241 min, 4 GB max: https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide/update/video. Safe zones for 9:16 (secondary, reflecting Meta's March 2026 unified guidance): 14% top, 35% bottom, 6% sides; keep critical elements in a centered 1080x1080 square: https://billo.app/blog/meta-ads-safe-zones/

**Length variants.** 6 s (bumper: logo sting plus one line), 15 s (non-skippable and X recommended), 30 s (paid hero), 60 s (organic hero and website). The 6 s and 15 s cuts are not trims; the Uber guidelines cut a dedicated wordmark animation to fit 6 s ads (https://www.behance.net/gallery/69157695/Uber-Motion-Guidelines). A launch shoot is typically planned to yield 30 s and 15 s spots, 6 s bumpers, vertical clips and looping GIFs: https://dmakproductions.com/blog/product-launch-video/

**Localized text layers and text expansion.** W3C's guidance (citing IBM): strings up to 10 characters expand 200 to 300%, 11 to 20 characters 180 to 200%, 21 to 30 characters 160 to 180%, over 70 characters about 130%; "the smaller the English text, the more likely it is to be squeezed into a small space": https://www.w3.org/International/articles/article-text-size. Design every super with headroom for the longest target language, and never bake text into rendered 3D.

**How studios structure the project.** After Effects: all supers as live text layers, driven by Essential Graphics controls or a spreadsheet, so a language swap is a data change (https://helpx.adobe.com/my_en/after-effects/desktop/motion-graphics/work-with-motion-graphics-templates/creating-motion-graphics-templates.html, https://aescripts.com/learn/post/localize-after-effects-projects-with-chatgpt-and-text2spreadsheet, https://cultextensions.com/2025/11/14/blog/). After Effects' text engine supports RTL (Hebrew, Arabic) and Indic scripts natively (https://helpx.adobe.com/ca/after-effects/desktop/add-text/create-and-edit-text-layers/creating-editing-text-layers.html). Typical structure: one master 16:9 comp at 3840x2160 or 1920x1080; the 3D and background renders delivered as oversized plates (or re-rendered per ratio) so 1:1, 4:5 and 9:16 comps re-frame rather than crop; text, logo and VO as separate layers per language; a "silent" variant with burned-in captions in the brand type; a clean master with no supers for press use; separate stems for VO, music and SFX so localized VO can be re-mixed. Deliver a spreadsheet of every on-screen string with translations, plus SRT per language.

## 5. Production process and roles

**Stages (agency or in-house are the same shape).** Brief, script and VO, storyboard, styleframes, animatic, animation, sound, grade, deliverables. Sources: https://runner.studio/blog/this-is-the-workflow-every-motion-design-studio-uses/ (eight stages with audio added "after picture-lock" and a final mix once audio is approved), https://www.motionmedia.com/mm-blog/motion-graphics-workflow-from-brief-to-final-render/, https://milanote.com/guide/motion-design-style-frames

**Sign-off gates.** "Animation is revised in stages: script, storyboard, styleframes, rough animation, final animation, sound, with each stage requiring review and sign-off before the next one begins." Structural changes after animatic sign-off restart the clock. Sources: https://pixune.com/blog/how-long-does-2d-animation-take/ and https://www.hocuspocus.studio/animation-production-timeline. For a regulated public company, add two gates that studios do not run by default: a legal and compliance gate on the locked script (before storyboards) and a second on the picture-locked cut with all supers (before sound and grade), plus IR sign-off on any number and on the release timing.

**Typical timeline for a 60 s piece** (Hocus Pocus Studio, 2D): script 5 to 7 days, storyboard 5 to 10 days, styleframes 3 to 7 days, animatic 2 to 5 days, animation 2 to 4 weeks, sound 3 to 5 days, delivery 1 to 2 days; 5 to 7 weeks total with 24 to 48 hour feedback turnarounds; 2 revision rounds at script, storyboard and styleframes, 1 to 2 at animation. Slippage causes: delayed feedback, scope creep, VO delays, "multiple stakeholders" adding 1 to 2 weeks. Source: https://www.hocuspocus.studio/animation-production-timeline. Other studios quote 6 to 8 weeks for 2D and 8 to 12 for frame-by-frame or heavy 3D (https://pixune.com/blog/how-long-does-2d-animation-take/, https://educationalvoice.co.uk/how-long-does-animation-production-take/). A public-company legal pass at two gates realistically adds 1 to 2 weeks; plan 8 to 10 weeks for a 3D brand-system piece.

**Budget bands (secondary sources, 2025 to 2026 studio pricing).** 60 s 2D explainer: agencies $12k to $15k, specialized studios $4k to $6k, freelancers $2k to $3k; North American studios $5k to $10k, premium 3D or custom-character work $10k to $25k+ for enterprise brand films (https://www.yumyumvideos.com/blog/how-much-does-animation-cost-per-minute-of-video/, https://vidico.com/news/how-much-does-animation-cost/, https://www.yansmedia.com/blog/explainer-video-cost). 3D per minute: $10k to $50k+ (https://creamyanimation.com/explainer-video-cost/). These bands are for explainers; a hero brand film from a studio of Brand New School's tier, with a bespoke 3D system, licensed music, VO talent, localization and cut-downs, sits well above them. Treat anything under $25k for a public-company hero with full versioning as a signal that something (rights, versions, or review rounds) has been left out.

**Review tooling.** Frame.io: "Click anywhere in a frame to leave precise, actionable feedback"; frame-accurate scrub; version stacks; Shares with custom branding and security settings; status labels "Needs Review," "In Progress," "Approved" that notify the project on change; Collections to organize what is awaiting review vs approved: https://frame.io/v4 and https://help.frame.io/en/articles/9105311-player-page-features. Enterprise: forensic watermarking (invisible per-viewer identifiers on every view, download and share): https://help.frame.io/en/articles/12091837-forensic-watermarking; SOC 2 Type 2 and ISO 27001 under Adobe: https://frame.io/security and https://www.adobe.com/cc-shared/assets/pdf/trust-center/ungated/whitepapers/creative-cloud/frame-io-security-overview.pdf. Workfront integration supports multi-stage approvals: https://help.frame.io/en/articles/12242607-adobe-workfront-integration-overview. For a pre-announcement asset, the review link should be watermarked, password-protected and download-disabled; the approved version's status change is the sign-off record.

**What Circle and Coinbase actually use (public record).**

- Circle: an in-house Creative Lab led by a VP Creative (Cameron Ewing) that partners with an outside studio for system-level work (Brand New School, end of 2023), and Mother Design for the USDC mark. The output was a scalable 3D system and a style guide rather than a single film, which is the pattern for a company that ships many launches a year. Sources: https://cameronewing.substack.com/p/refreshing-the-circle-brand-system, https://www.brandnewschool.com/work/circle-illustrated-branding-system, https://lbbonline.com/news/Mother-Design-Brand-Identity-Circles-USDC-Stablecoin. Circle's earlier 2020 rebrand illustration work is credited to Mark Grambau: https://www.markgrambau.com/projects/circle-2020-rebrand-illustrations. Arc mainnet (2026-09-16) launched with a press release, an 8-K-style disclosure pattern, and a livestream on @arc rather than a standalone hero film in the release itself (https://www.circle.com/pressroom/circle-launches-arc-mainnet-an-economic-operating-system-for-the-internet). No public credits were found for a specific Circle launch video; that is a gap, not evidence of in-house production.
- Coinbase: identity and motion system by Moniker (2022); Super Bowl 2022 QR-code spot created by Accenture Interactive after an in-house-led brief, with The Martin Agency publicly disputing credit. Sources: https://monikersf.com/project/coinbase/, https://www.thedrum.com/news/2022/02/22/the-martin-agency-s-ceo-claims-coinbase-took-inspiration-its-qr-code-super-bowl, https://decrypt.co/93559/coinbase-ceo-downplays-role-ad-agency-super-bowl-commercial. Lesson for credits: agree the public credit line in the SOW.

Guess, flagged as such: at Circle's scale the hero launch video is most likely produced in-house by Creative Lab using the Brand New School system and toolkit, with outside studios used for system refreshes and peak load. No source confirms this.

## 6. Distribution: launch day and the asset list

**Sequence (PR practice, secondary sources).** Embargoed release to Tier 1 journalists 1 to 3 weeks out; courtesy reminder 24 hours before the embargo lifts; wire distribution between 6 and 9 AM ET; corporate blog post publishes simultaneously; social posts across LinkedIn, the CEO's account and the company X account; direct pitches 10 AM to 2 PM local: https://www.instantpress.co/blog/how-to-get-press-for-a-new-product, https://presscable.com/insights/how-to-embargo-a-press-release-tips-examples/, https://growpublicrelations.com/product-launch-pr-strategy/. For a registrant, layer the securities sequence on top: 8-K furnished (if IR deems it material) at or before the wire time; the video and every social post carry only facts already in the release; executives post only after the release is live and only on channels previously identified to investors (https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2013-2013-51htm).

**Per channel.**
- Website hero: 16:9 master, autoplay muted with visible pause (WCAG 1.4.2 and 2.2.2), a poster frame, `prefers-reduced-motion` fallback to the poster, captions on, transcript linked.
- YouTube: 16:9 master at the encoding spec above, custom thumbnail 1280x720 under 2 MB, human-checked SRT, optional AD track, end-screen linking to the release; a Premiere can be scheduled to the embargo time.
- X: 16:9 or 1:1 under 2:20 (organic non-Premium under 140 s), captions burned in, first frame designed as the thumbnail; 9:16 for the follow-up.
- LinkedIn: 1:1 or 4:5 native upload with SRT, sound-off first.
- Instagram and Facebook: 9:16 Reels cut respecting the 14/35/6% safe zone, 4:5 Feed.
- Press kit: the Circle pressroom pattern (logo zips in light and dark, brand use policy PDF, executive headshots and bios) extended for the launch: https://www.circle.com/pressroom.

**Asset list PR teams expect** (synthesized from https://www.prezly.com/academy/press-kit-101-what-to-include-to-get-earned-media-coverage, https://www.agilitypr.com/pr-news/content-media-relations/how-to-create-an-amazing-press-or-media-kit-9-essentials-to-include/, https://dmakproductions.com/blog/product-launch-video/):
- Hero video 16:9 at 1080p and 4K, ProRes master plus H.264 web master; clean version with no supers; 60 / 30 / 15 / 6 s cuts; 1:1, 4:5, 9:16 versions; silent-with-captions versions.
- Poster frame and thumbnail (1280x720 and 1920x1080; also 1080x1080 and 1080x1920).
- 6 to 10 stills at 4K, and 2 to 4 looping GIFs or short MP4 loops (under 5 s) for embeds and email.
- SRT and VTT per language; full transcript as text; audio description track or extended transcript.
- Music cue sheet and license PDFs; font license confirmation; third-party mark permissions log.
- Logos (light and dark, SVG and PNG), product mark, partner logo lockups with partner approvals.
- Boilerplate, forward-looking statements legend, product-level regulatory disclaimer, press contact.
- A one-page usage note for media: what may be re-cut, credit line, expiry (if any) on music.

## Pre-launch checklist (pass / fail)

Each item is binary. A "fail" blocks release. Owners in brackets.

**Securities and comms**
1. Every number, partner name, date and metric in the video already appears verbatim in the press release or a prior public filing. [IR]
2. The release (and 8-K if IR deems the news material) is timed to publish at or before the first public post of the video; the video's publish time is later or equal. [IR, Comms]
3. Executives will post only from channels previously disclosed to investors as distribution channels, and only after the release is live. [IR, Legal]
4. The script has been reviewed for forward-looking statements; each remaining one is either rewritten as present-tense fact or covered by an on-screen legend and a link to the release with the 10-K risk-factor reference. [Legal]
5. No line in VO or supers implies FDIC insurance, US government backing, legal tender status, or "as safe as a bank." No dollar-bill, seal, flag or Treasury-style imagery adjacent to the stablecoin. [Legal, Creative]
6. If any broker-dealer affiliate's product is shown or the video will be used by one: a registered principal has approved the retail communication before first use, and the approval is logged with name and date. [BD Compliance]
7. Crypto assets are not described as cash or cash equivalents; product-vs-affiliate offerings are differentiated; no comparison to stocks or cash without a stated basis. [Legal]
8. If the entity is a NYDFS licensee and the video targets NY residents: licensee name and the NYDFS legend appear; the video, script and all cut-downs are archived for seven years. [Compliance]
9. Product-level regulatory disclaimer text is present where the release carries one (e.g. "has not been reviewed or approved by NYDFS or any other regulatory authority"). [Legal]
10. A claims file exists: every on-screen or spoken statistic has a dated source and a named owner; disclosures for each claim are legible at the fastest cut-down's on-screen duration and, for audio claims, are also spoken. [Legal, Producer]

**Rights**
11. Music: sync and master licenses in hand, covering all media including paid social and broadcast, worldwide, in perpetuity, with cut-down and localized re-edit rights; cue sheet delivered. [Producer]
12. Fonts: every typeface used is covered by a license tier that permits video, motion graphics and (if applicable) broadcast; the license holder is the company, not only the studio. [Producer]
13. Third-party marks: a rights log lists each external logo or name, its owner's policy URL, the basis for use (nominative or written permission), and frame ranges; Ethereum Foundation and similar owners with permission-only policies have written clearance for logo use. [Legal]
14. Partner logos: each partner's comms team has approved the exact frame(s) and lockup, not just the release copy. [Comms]
15. VO talent, stock footage, 3D assets and any AI-generated elements have written usage rights covering the same media and term as the music. [Producer]
16. Credit line for the studio or agency is agreed in writing in the SOW. [Producer]

**Brand**
17. Logo clear space and minimum size hold in every ratio and every cut-down at delivery resolution (Circle: 2x clear space, 120 px web minimum; USDC/EURC 24 px minimum; Arc inner-arch 1x). [Brand]
18. Logo animation, easing family, duration tiers and motion direction match the brand motion guidelines; no bounce, stretch or sudden stops unless the system allows them. [Brand]
19. Type, color tokens and material library are the approved set; color is graded and delivered in BT.709 for SDR. [Brand, Post]
20. A 6 s and a 15 s cut use a dedicated logo sting timed to those lengths, not a trim of the 60 s reveal. [Creative]

**Accessibility**
21. Human-checked captions (SRT and VTT) exist per language; accuracy checked against the final VO; 32-character lines and roughly 150 to 160 wpm; not obscuring on-screen text. [Post]
22. Audio description track or extended transcript delivered; a plain-text transcript is published with the video. [Post]
23. All burned-in text and captions meet 4.5:1 contrast (3:1 for large text) against the actual frames behind them across the whole shot, not just the first frame. [Design]
24. The final master of every version passes a flash analysis (PEAT for web, HardingFPA for broadcast): no more than three flashes per second, red-flash threshold respected, including transitions, light passes and logo hits. [Post]
25. Website hero embed: autoplay muted, visible pause within reach, honors `prefers-reduced-motion`, captions available, transcript linked. [Web]

**Versioning and platform**
26. Deliverables exist for 16:9, 1:1, 4:5 and 9:16 with content re-framed (not center-cropped) and critical elements inside each platform's safe zone (9:16: 14% top, 35% bottom, 6% sides). [Post]
27. Silent-with-captions versions exist for every ratio; the argument reads with sound off. [Creative]
28. Each platform file meets spec: YouTube H.264 High, AAC-LC 48 kHz, 8 to 12 Mbps at 1080p, BT.709; X under 512 MB and 140 s (organic) or 2:20 (ads); LinkedIn MP4 under 500 MB; Meta under 4 GB. [Post]
29. Localized versions render from live text layers with headroom for text expansion (plan for 130% to 300% depending on string length); RTL languages verified; no baked-in text in 3D plates. [Post]
30. Master and stems archived: ProRes clean master, VO/music/SFX stems, project files, and the string spreadsheet. [Post]

**Process and records**
31. Legal and compliance signed off at two gates: locked script before storyboards, and picture lock with all supers before sound and grade; both sign-offs recorded in the review tool with status "Approved." [Producer]
32. Pre-release review links were watermarked, password-protected and download-disabled; the approved version is the one uploaded (checksum matches). [Producer]
33. Press kit is complete: masters, cut-downs, poster frames, thumbnails, stills, GIFs, transcripts, SRTs, logos, license and permission log, boilerplate, legend, disclaimer, press contact, usage note. [Comms]
34. Launch-day run sheet lists every post with channel, timestamp, owner, asset filename and caption text, sequenced after the release timestamp. [Comms]
