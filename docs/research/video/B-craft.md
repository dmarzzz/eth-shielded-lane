# B. Craft gap: studio-grade 3D motion vs a good real-time three.js render, and what Remotion + three.js can close

Research date: 2026-09-18. Every claim below carries its URL. Effort estimates in section 6 and the ranked list are my own estimates and are labelled as such. Inferences that go beyond what a source states are marked "(inference)".

---

## 1. Image quality

### What the studio pipeline does
- Offline renderers are path tracers. Cycles is "a ray-trace based production render engine" using unidirectional path tracing with multiple importance sampling, runs on CUDA/OptiX/HIP/OneAPI/Metal, and has built-in denoising. https://www.blender.org/features/rendering/ and https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/2.91/render/cycles/introduction.html
- Light is physical: area lights cast soft shadows, glass refracts with volume absorption, and global illumination comes for free from bounces. Blender's glTF pipeline carries transmission, volume, IOR, clearcoat, sheen, specular and emissive strength as first-class material properties. https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/3.6/addons/import_export/scene_gltf2.html
- Color management is a view transform, not a shader constant. Blender 4.0 made AgX the default; it "offers 16.5 stops of dynamic range and unsaturates highly exposed colors to mimic film's natural response to light." Filmic is deprecated; ACES needs its own OCIO config. https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.0/render/color_management.html and https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/4.0/color_management/
- Lens effects are used with restraint. School of Motion's guide on photographic effects in 3D: bloom "a little goes a long way," apply it only to the highest highlights; chromatic aberration is something "many artists overuse"; film grain is fine "if it's not overdone"; shallow DOF is for portrait/macro framing, and vignetting done in-render recovers edge highlights that a post vignette only darkens. https://schoolofmotion.com/blog/how-to-mimic-photographic-effects
- Highlights desaturate toward white as they get brighter ("a nice white hot core that falls off to a saturated color"); this is what AgX and ACES-style transforms do automatically and what a naive sRGB clamp does not. Same source.

### What three.js can do today
- MeshPhysicalMaterial has transmission, thickness, attenuationDistance/attenuationColor, ior (1.0 to 2.333), dispersion, iridescence, sheen, clearcoat, anisotropy and specularIntensity. The docs warn it "has a higher performance cost per pixel" and that you should "always specify an environment map." https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshPhysicalMaterial.html
- Transmission is rasterized: opaque objects are rendered to a texture first, then transmissive surfaces sample it, so stacked glass does not see other glass. https://discourse.threejs.org/t/multi-layered-transmission/43634
- drei's MeshTransmissionMaterial adds "chromatic aberration, noise-based roughness blur, (primitive) anisotropic blur," can see other transmissive objects, but costs "an additional render pass of the scene" per material unless you share a buffer. http://drei.docs.pmnd.rs/shaders/mesh-transmission-material
- Area lights exist (RectAreaLight) but "there is no shadow support" and only PBR materials react. https://threejs.org/docs/pages/RectAreaLight.html Shadow-map research for area lights is still an open issue. https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/issues/14161
- HDRI lighting works and is the single biggest realism lever; equirectangular HDR must be run through PMREMGenerator to light MeshStandard/Physical materials. https://discourse.threejs.org/t/hdr-environment-map-doesnt-work-unless-i-convert-it-to-a-pmrem/13378
- Tone mapping: AgXToneMapping shipped in r160 (PRs #27366, #27403, #27409). https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/releases/tag/r160 and https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/issues/27362 ACESFilmic in three is "modified to accommodate a brighter viewing environment." https://threejs.org/docs/pages/module-ACESFilmicToneMappingShader.html
- Anti-aliasing beyond MSAA: SSAARenderPass re-renders the scene 2^n times with camera jitter (default sampleLevel 4 = 16 samples, RGBA16F accumulation). https://threejs.org/docs/pages/SSAARenderPass.html TAARenderPass "uses no reprojection so it is not a TRAA implementation" and only accumulates when the scene is static. https://threejs.org/docs/pages/TAARenderPass.html
- Post: pmndrs/postprocessing ships SMAA, bloom, depth of field (disc bokeh), SSAO, chromatic aberration, noise, vignette, LUT, tone mapping, with automatic pass merging; it has no TAA and no motion blur. https://github.com/pmndrs/postprocessing Motion blur exists as a WebGPU velocity-buffer example in three.js, https://threejs.org/examples/webgpu_postprocessing_motion_blur.html , as gkjohnson's per-object velocity pass, https://gkjohnson.github.io/threejs-sandbox/motionBlurPass/webgl_postprocessing_perobjectmotionblur.html , and in realism-effects (SSGI, SSR, TRAA, motion blur, HBAO) which lists open limitations (no orthographic camera, incomplete transparency, TRAA dilation issues). https://github.com/0beqz/realism-effects
- Real path tracing in the browser: three-gpu-pathtracer (WebGL 2) supports MeshStandard/MeshPhysical only, GGX, environment maps with MIS, rectangular and circular area lights, IES spot lights, DOF, volumetric fog, transmission, dispersion, iridescence, sheen, clearcoat, attenuation, tiled rendering, renderScale, bounces (default 10), and has an animation/video rendering demo. It does not support instanced geometry or interleaved buffers, and renderSample() renders one sample per call. https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-gpu-pathtracer A WebGPU branch is active (maxBounces rename, docs, "fix render video demo", merged 2026-09-14). https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-gpu-pathtracer/pull/851

### How Remotion renders, and what that allows
- Remotion renders deterministically frame by frame in headless Chromium: seek to a timestamp, run the React tree, screenshot. https://ayanamizuta.com/en/posts/remotion-how-to-rendering/ and https://www.remotion.dev/docs/renderer/render-frames
- ThreeCanvas forces frameloop to 'never' and re-renders only through advance(); animation must be driven by useCurrentFrame(), never useFrame(). https://www.remotion.dev/docs/three-canvas and https://www.remotion.dev/docs/three
- --gl: from Remotion 5.0 angle is the default locally with automatic SwiftShader fallback; angle-egl for cloud GPU instances; swangle on Lambda/Cloud Run. Memory leaks are documented with angle, so split long renders. https://www.remotion.dev/docs/gl-options Chrome for Testing (--chrome-mode=chrome-for-testing) is the mode for "GPU-accelerated rendering environment on Linux" and is unavailable on Lambda/Cloud Run. https://www.remotion.dev/docs/miscellaneous/chrome-headless-shell "Compute instances in the cloud do not have a GPU." https://www.remotion.dev/docs/performance
- --scale: up to 16x; server-side it changes the browser's deviceScaleFactor, and text, SVG and canvas/WebGL (via pixelDensity) upscale cleanly; embedded video does not. A 1920x1080 composition with --scale=2 gives a 3840x2160 master. https://www.remotion.dev/docs/scaling
- Output: h264 default CRF 18, h265, vp8/vp9, av1, prores; --pixel-format (yuv444p, 10-bit), --color-space bt709/bt2020, --image-format png for lossless frames, --jpeg-quality otherwise. https://www.remotion.dev/docs/encoding and https://www.remotion.dev/docs/cli/render ProRes 4444 / 4444-xq with --image-format=png --pixel-format=yuva444p10le is the documented mastering path. https://www.remotion.dev/docs/transparent-videos
- Motion blur: @remotion/motion-blur CameraMotionBlur renders sub-frames (default 10 samples, shutterAngle 180) and averages them, "destructive to colors," children must be absolutely positioned. https://www.remotion.dev/docs/motion-blur/camera-motion-blur Trail duplicates children with a per-layer time offset. https://www.remotion.dev/docs/motion-blur/trail

### Viability verdicts
- Frame-accurate 4K high-DPR renders: viable. --scale=2 plus PNG frames plus ProRes 4444 is the documented path. (inference: R3F's default dpr follows window.devicePixelRatio, which Remotion sets via deviceScaleFactor, so the WebGL buffer follows scale; verify with a test still.)
- SSAA inside Remotion: viable and deterministic because every frame is a fresh on-demand render; use SSAARenderPass (or renderer MSAA + SMAA). TAARenderPass accumulation is not usable because Remotion captures one advance() per frame (inference from the two docs above).
- Path tracing inside Remotion: technically possible on a local machine with a real GPU under --gl=angle, by calling renderSample() N times inside a delayRender() before the capture (inference). It is not viable on Lambda/Cloud Run (no GPU, swangle software rendering) and would multiply render time by the sample count. Use it for hero stills or short plates, not the whole piece.

**What this means for a Remotion/three.js piece:** you can close lighting (HDRI + PMREM), color (AgX/ACES + BT.709), anti-aliasing (SSAA at 2x scale), post (bloom, DOF, grain, vignette, CA) and delivery (ProRes 4444 master) inside the pipeline; you cannot get true area-light shadows, multi-bounce GI, or stacked refraction without a path tracer, and a path tracer is only practical locally on GPU for a handful of shots.

## 2. Motion craft

- The 12 principles that carry into motion graphics: squash and stretch, anticipation, staging, follow-through and overlapping action, slow in and slow out, arcs, secondary action, timing, exaggeration, appeal. Slow in/slow out "is arguably the single most important principle for motion graphics." https://www.creativebloq.com/advice/understand-the-12-principles-of-animation and https://www.cgspectrum.com/blog/12-principles-of-animation
- Default eases are the tell. School of Motion: "default easy-ease keyframes are rarely exactly what you need"; professionals "control what happens between the keyframes." https://schoolofmotion.com/blog/graph-editor-after-effects Overshoot goes past the target and settles; bounce resolves with decaying rebounds; the speed graph is where stylized eases and music-matched energy are shaped. https://mtmograph.com/blogs/tools/the-bounce-and-overshoot-animation-trick-every-motion-designer-should-know and https://olafmotion.com/motion-knowledge/speed-graph-vs-value-graph-after-effects/
- Camera language: a dolly changes perspective and produces parallax; a zoom does not. https://www.masterclass.com/articles/guide-to-camera-moves Rack focus shifts attention between foreground and background. https://vidpros.com/camera-movements/ Parallax layers give depth with foreground/background moving at different rates and are a standard explainer device. https://motiondesign.school/blog/parallax-in-after-effects/
- How studios stage a product or system piece: three movements, hero reveal (slow, intrigue-building, edges lit before details), details (macro close-ups, light "drawing lines" across surfaces), product in context. https://www.manfrotto.com/global-uk/stories/how-to-shoot-cinematic-tech-videos-like-apple/ 3D hero films typically run reveal, design story, key features, closing frame for landing page and press kit. https://maverickframe.com/blog/what-is-product-animation/
- Studio process: ManvsMachine's Simon Holmedal on finding clarity early ("if you find clarity early on, it often informs most of the problems you encounter later on"), intentional restraint ("why compromise?" on one direction, dark/moody or bright/colorful), motivated details, darker believable environments over literal ones, and sound design as a major part of impact. https://motionographer.com/2016/05/02/interview-man-vs-machines-simon-holmedal/ Scholar's breakdown: boardomatics "for a sense of motion and timing" before 3D, one camera idea (infinite zoom) carried the whole piece, three months total. https://dev.motionographer.com/2022/11/20/project-breakdown-with-scholar/
- Pacing numbers: cuts every 1 to 2 s read as energy; every 4 to 6 s gives room for emotional beats or complex explanation; "when brands say the pace is too slow, they often mean shots linger too long after viewers have absorbed the information." https://www.visla.us/blog/guides/video-editing-pacing-what-it-is-and-how-to-control-it/ Explainer convention is 60 to 90 s at about 150 spoken words per minute. https://www.yansmedia.com/blog/how-long-should-my-explainer-video-be
- Premium vs explainer pacing (inference from the sources above): premium pieces hold a single camera idea across long shots, spend the first 3 to 5 s on a reveal with nothing to read, and let each beat resolve (build, reveal, hold, resolve) before the next; explainer pacing cuts on the information rate with new text every 2 to 3 s. The premium tell is the hold: a shot stays a beat longer than needed to read, and the motion settles rather than stops.

**What this means for a Remotion/three.js piece:** the whole of this section is closable in code at zero render cost: replace default eases with asymmetric bezier or spring curves, add anticipation and overshoot, stagger secondary elements 2 to 6 frames behind primaries, drive one slow dolly with parallax instead of zooms, and structure the timeline as build, reveal, hold, resolve with explicit hold frames.

## 3. Typography and information design in motion

- Type in mograph: "Think about what you want to say and then how you want to say it"; sans-serifs "are usually easier to read in MoGraph"; kerning, tracking and leading are the controls that separate a professional result. https://schoolofmotion.com/blog/fonts-typefaces-typography-for-motion-design
- Safe areas: title safe is the inner 80% of the frame, action safe the inner 90%. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safe_area_(television) and https://handcraftedpen.co.uk/title-safe-area/
- Phone legibility numbers (1080x1920 canvas): primary text 48 to 72 px bold, secondary 32 to 40 px, minimum readable 36 px; 5 to 8 words per overlay card, at most 2 lines visible, minimum 1.5 s per line (a 10-word line needs about 2.5 s); entrance animations 150 to 250 ms; "avoid complex kinetic typography, letter-by-letter animations"; 4.5:1 contrast or a 60 to 80% backing panel; keep the bottom 250 to 350 px clear of platform UI. https://www.rocketshiphq.com/text-overlays-video-ads-mobile/
- Kinetic type best practice is to limit the number of animated words on screen at once and let line breaks lead the eye. https://www.todaymade.com/blog/kinetic-typography-examples
- Studio pieces keep text sparse: the Apple-style structure puts a reveal with no text first and uses captions as punctuation, not narration. https://www.manfrotto.com/global-uk/stories/how-to-shoot-cinematic-tech-videos-like-apple/
- Label choreography (inference from the rocketship numbers and the easing sources): labels enter on a 150 to 250 ms fade-plus-rise tied to the object they name, hold for at least 1.5 s per line, and exit before the camera moves rather than during it; letter-by-letter animation is an explainer tell.

**What this means for a Remotion/three.js piece:** fully closable in code: one family with 2 or 3 weights, tracking tightened at display sizes, labels choreographed as short enters and exits, a hard cap of 5 to 8 words per card and 2 lines, everything inside the inner 80%, and a 36 px floor at 1080 wide checked on a phone.

## 4. Sound

- Role: "most actions generate sound," so silent motion reads as unnatural, but sound is deployed selectively; small percussive hits synced to specific elements direct attention while other motion stays unadorned; effects "explain what we see" and should sit "in the same spirit as the music"; imprecise sound reads as a flaw, not a style. https://www.nicolastiteux.com/en/blog/sound-design-for-motion-design/
- Restraint: "Silence is often the missing ingredient... A clean pause before a product reveal can do more than another riser or impact hit"; overloaded short-form audio "usually feels smaller, not bigger." https://www.remotionvideo.com/blog/what-is-sound-design
- Holmedal names sound design as a significant part of ManvsMachine's impact. https://motionographer.com/2016/05/02/interview-man-vs-machines-simon-holmedal/
- Loudness targets: YouTube −14 LUFS, −1 dBTP, "turns down only; never boosts"; Apple −16 LUFS; EBU R128 broadcast −23 LUFS, ±0.5 LU, −1 dBTP; ATSC A/85 (US broadcast, CALM Act) −24 LKFS, −2 dBTP; TikTok/Instagram/Meta have no published target and use adaptive loudness, so −1 dBTP and about −14 is the safe default; X has no published target in this source. https://www.forasoft.com/learn/audio-for-video/articles-audio/lufs-targets-per-platform-2026 and https://www.peak-studios.de/en/youtube-audio-richtlinien-streaming-2025/
- How to hit it from the CLI: two-pass ffmpeg loudnorm, measure with print_format=json then apply with measured_* values and linear=true. https://dev.to/masonwritescode/two-pass-loudness-normalization-with-ffmpeg-loudnorm-the-right-way-1nm3
- Licensing: Epidemic Sound Pro ($16.99/mo annual) grants perpetual rights for productions completed during the subscription, broadcast only on enterprise; Artlist Max Business ($399/mo annual) covers client transfer, broadcast and paid social with capped indemnity; Musicbed is per-project sync with "$10,000 per unauthorized use or 10x fee" liquidated damages for unlogged use; Soundstripe single-use digital license $199 flat. All require registering/clearlisting the project before cancelling. https://thepostflow.com/post-production/video-editing/music-subscriptions-compared/ Musicbed sits at the high-end film/advertising end. https://www.creatoraidaily.com/blog/epidemic-sound-vs-artlist-vs-musicbed-youtube-creators-2026/
- Silent-first: publishers reported "85 percent of Facebook video is watched without sound" and redesigned around on-screen text. https://digiday.com/media/silent-world-facebook-video/ Chrome permits muted autoplay only; hero loops must be muted. https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Learn_web_development/Extensions/Performance/video
- Whether launch videos are silent-first: on feeds, yes in practice (muted autoplay plus the stat above), which is why the piece has to read with sound off; sound still matters for YouTube, the site player with a click, and the studio version. (inference)

**What this means for a Remotion/three.js piece:** sound is fully closable and cheap: one licensed track (Artlist Pro/Max or a single-use license if the piece runs as paid social), 5 to 8 sparse SFX on reveals and label hits, a deliberate silence before the reveal, two-pass loudnorm to −14 LUFS / −1 dBTP for the web master and −23 or −24 if broadcast ever happens, and the visual piece must already work muted.

## 5. Delivery specs

- YouTube (primary source): MP4, moov atom at front, no edit lists; H.264 High profile, progressive, 2 consecutive B-frames, closed GOP at half frame rate, CABAC, VBR, 4:2:0; AAC-LC 48 kHz, 384 kbps stereo; upload at the frame rate you produced (24/25/30/48/50/60); BT.709 for SDR; SDR bitrates 1080p 8 Mbps (12 at high frame rate), 1440p 16/24, 2160p 35 to 45 / 53 to 68 Mbps; HDR 2160p 44 to 56 / 66 to 85. https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1722171?hl=en YouTube re-encodes to VP9/AV1/H.264, so upload with headroom. https://swarmify.com/blog/what-you-need-to-know-about-the-video-bitrate/
- LinkedIn ads (primary source): MP4, 3 s to 30 min, 75 KB to 500 MB, 30 fps recommended, 360x360 min to 1920x1920 max, ratios 4:5, 9:16, 16:9, 1:1, AAC or MPEG4 audio. https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/ads/sponsored-content/video-ads/specs Organic LinkedIn video: up to 4096x2304, 5 GB, up to 30 Mbps, 60 fps allowed, 30 recommended. https://sproutsocial.com/insights/social-media-video-specs-guide/
- X: MP4 or MOV, 512 MB (standard) or larger for Premium, 0.5 to 140 s standard, 1920x1200 max, 16:9 or 1:1, 30 or 60 fps, 5,000 to 25,000 kbps recommended (8 to 12 Mbps for 1080p30 is typical). https://sproutsocial.com/insights/social-media-video-specs-guide/ and https://www.nemovideo.com/blog/twitter-video-specs-guide-2026 X has no 4K path; 1080p is the ceiling in practice.
- Captions: X composer has "Upload caption file (.srt)" next to Tag people and Media Studio has a Subtitles tab; LinkedIn "Select Caption" attaches an SRT. https://help.x.com/en/using-x/upload-caption-srt-file and https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a552177/add-closed-captions-to-videos-on-linkedin?lang=en Burned-in captions are the fallback where SRT is not accepted (website hero, some ad placements). (inference)
- Website hero loop: 3 to 8 s, muted, autoplay, playsinline, poster image; 720p for most sites, test 1080p; 1,000 to 2,500 kbps and under about 3 MB; H.264 MP4 with an optional WebM source after measuring the saving; 24 to 30 fps. https://swarmify.com/blog/best-video-format-for-website/ and https://zatta.link/en/web/video-tag-and-recommended-file-spec.html and https://designtlc.com/how-to-optimize-a-silent-background-video-for-your-websites-hero-area/
- Masters: ProRes 4444 is "approximately 330 Mbps for 4:4:4 sources at 1920x1080 and 29.97 fps," 4444 XQ about 500 Mbps, 422 HQ about 220 Mbps; "Apple ProRes 4444 and Apple ProRes 4444 XQ are ideal for the exchange of motion graphics media because they are virtually lossless" and are the only profiles with alpha. https://support.apple.com/en-us/102207 422 HQ is enough for broadcast delivery at 1080p if no heavy grade or VFX follows. https://larryjordan.com/articles/pick-the-right-version-of-prores/
- Frame rate: 24 for a cinematic, mood-led cut; 30 is the practical default for explain/sell/teach content on social; 60 only when the hook is motion itself; converting between rates introduces judder. https://www.adorama.com/alc/understanding-frame-rates-when-to-use-24-30-or-60-fps/ and https://www.capcut.com/create/video-frame-rates-guide
- 4K vs 1080p: YouTube and the website benefit from a 4K master (YouTube serves higher-bitrate ladders for 4K uploads); X and LinkedIn ads cap at 1920 on the long side, so deliver 1080p derivatives from the 4K master. (inference from the specs above)

**What this means for a Remotion/three.js piece:** render one 3840x2160 ProRes 4444 master at the chosen frame rate (24 or 30, decide once), then derive H.264 High 4:2:0 BT.709 MP4s: YouTube 4K at 45 Mbps, X/LinkedIn 1080p at 10 to 12 Mbps under 500 MB, a 720p/1080p muted 3 to 8 s hero loop under 3 MB, plus an SRT for X and LinkedIn.

## 6. Pipeline options with effort estimates

Effort figures below are my estimates for one developer/designer who already has the Remotion + three.js scene working; they are not sourced.

### (a) Stay in Remotion/three.js and add HDRI, post, SSAA, DOF, motion blur, 4K, sound
- Lighting: load an EXR/HDR through PMREMGenerator as scene.environment, keep one directional light with a soft shadow map, and optionally one RectAreaLight for a rim (no shadows). https://discourse.threejs.org/t/hdr-environment-map-doesnt-work-unless-i-convert-it-to-a-pmrem/13378 and https://threejs.org/docs/pages/RectAreaLight.html Estimate: half a day.
- Color: renderer.toneMapping = AgXToneMapping (r160+), sRGB output, --color-space bt709. https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/releases/tag/r160 and https://www.remotion.dev/docs/cli/render Estimate: an hour.
- AA and resolution: EffectComposer with SSAARenderPass (sampleLevel 3 or 4) or MSAA + SMAA, rendered at --scale=2, PNG frames. https://threejs.org/docs/pages/SSAARenderPass.html and https://www.remotion.dev/docs/scaling Estimate: half a day plus 4x to 16x render time.
- Post: pmndrs postprocessing bloom (high threshold), DepthOfField, vignette, low-amplitude noise, sub-pixel chromatic aberration. https://github.com/pmndrs/postprocessing Estimate: half a day.
- Motion blur: CameraMotionBlur with 6 to 10 samples (multiplies frame cost by the sample count and shifts colors), or a velocity-buffer pass. https://www.remotion.dev/docs/motion-blur/camera-motion-blur Estimate: hours to wire, plus render time.
- Glass: MeshTransmissionMaterial with a shared transmission buffer, thickness, attenuationColor and a small dispersion value. http://drei.docs.pmnd.rs/shaders/mesh-transmission-material Estimate: one day of look-dev.
- Sound: licensed track, sparse SFX, two-pass loudnorm. Estimate: one day plus license cost.
- Master and derivatives: ProRes 4444 at 4K, then ffmpeg encodes per platform. https://www.remotion.dev/docs/transparent-videos Estimate: hours.
- Total: about 4 to 6 working days plus render time. Local GPU required (angle); Lambda is software-rendered. https://www.remotion.dev/docs/gl-options
- Ceiling: no area-light shadows, no multi-bounce GI, single-layer refraction, no real TRAA. Path-traced hero stills via three-gpu-pathtracer are possible locally as a separate step. https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-gpu-pathtracer

### (b) Export the scene to glTF, render in Blender (Cycles or EEVEE), composite in After Effects or DaVinci
- three.js GLTFExporter to Blender for Cycles is an established path ("magazine quality renders"). https://discourse.threejs.org/t/cool-things-you-can-do-with-gltfexporter-blender-cycles-rendering/7112 Blender's importer carries PBR extensions, punctual lights, cameras and keyframe animation; custom shaders and non-Principled node trees do not survive. https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/3.6/addons/import_export/scene_gltf2.html
- What you gain: true path tracing with area-light shadows, GI, volume absorption in glass, proper motion blur and DOF in the integrator, AgX by default, denoising, GPU rendering. https://www.blender.org/features/rendering/ and https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.0/render/color_management.html
- What you lose: everything driven procedurally in React (data-driven labels, easing code, Remotion timeline) has to be re-authored as Blender keyframes or baked into glTF animation, and typography is better done in AE/Resolve over the render as a separate layer.
- Estimate: 1 to 2 weeks for an existing 30 to 60 s scene (export and material repair 1 to 2 days, relighting and look-dev 2 to 3 days, camera and animation re-timing 1 to 2 days, render time on a single GPU from hours to days depending on samples, compositing and type 2 to 3 days). Blender's own 3D timeline guides quote 8 weeks to 3 months for studio-produced 3D. https://www.b2w.tv/blog/how-much-does-animation-cost
- EEVEE is the middle road: real-time previs of Cycles shading with soft shadows and screen-space effects, still inside Blender's color pipeline. https://www.blender.org/features/rendering/

### (c) Commission a studio
- Top tier: Buck "from ~$100,000," 12 to 24 weeks; Giant Ant from ~$75,000, 8 to 16 weeks; Ordinary Folk from ~$50,000, 6 to 14 weeks; mid-tier studios from ~$3,500 to $8,000 at 4 to 8 weeks. https://knowlify.com/articles/best-motion-graphics-studios ManvsMachine is positioned alongside Buck for high-craft 3D brand films (no rate published). https://www.b2w.tv/blog/motion-graphics-companies
- Per-minute 3D rates: freelancers $1,000 to $5,000/min, boutique studios $5,000 to $15,000/min, full-service $15,000 to $50,000+/min; cinematic/broadcast tier $30,000 to $50,000+/min on 12 to 24 weeks; two revision rounds included, each extra round adds 10 to 20%; rush "can double total production cost." https://framesixty.com/how-much-does-3d-animation-cost/ b2w: entry 3D $5,000/min, mid $10,000/min, high-end $20,000 to $50,000+/min, 8 weeks to 3 months. https://www.b2w.tv/blog/how-much-does-animation-cost
- For a 30 to 60 s piece: mid-tier freelance or boutique $5k to $25k in 4 to 8 weeks; Buck/ManvsMachine tier six figures in 3 to 6 months. (inference combining the two sources)

**What this means for a Remotion/three.js piece:** option (a) closes most of the visible gap in under a week and keeps the data-driven, re-renderable pipeline; option (b) is the only way to get true GI, area shadows and stacked refraction and costs one to two weeks plus a fork of the animation into Blender; option (c) buys taste and time you do not have, at $50k+ and months.

## Ranked: ten changes with the highest quality per effort (my ranking)

1. **Color pipeline**: AgX (or ACES) tone mapping, sRGB output, BT.709 flagged in the encode, highlights allowed to roll to white. Hours. https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/releases/tag/r160 https://www.remotion.dev/docs/cli/render
2. **HDRI environment through PMREM on every physical material**, one soft-shadow key, optional RectAreaLight rim. Half a day. https://discourse.threejs.org/t/hdr-environment-map-doesnt-work-unless-i-convert-it-to-a-pmrem/13378
3. **Easing and timing pass**: replace default eases with asymmetric curves and springs, add anticipation and overshoot, stagger secondaries, add explicit holds (build, reveal, hold, resolve). One to two days, zero render cost, largest perceived jump. https://schoolofmotion.com/blog/graph-editor-after-effects
4. **4K master via --scale=2 with SSAA and PNG frames, ProRes 4444 out**, platform MP4s derived from it. Half a day plus render time. https://www.remotion.dev/docs/scaling https://www.remotion.dev/docs/transparent-videos
5. **Camera language**: one slow dolly with parallax layers and a rack focus via DOF instead of zooms and cuts. One day. https://www.masterclass.com/articles/guide-to-camera-moves
6. **Restrained post stack**: bloom on top highlights only, DOF, vignette, 2 to 3% grain, sub-pixel chromatic aberration. Half a day. https://schoolofmotion.com/blog/how-to-mimic-photographic-effects https://github.com/pmndrs/postprocessing
7. **Type system and label choreography**: one family, 2 or 3 weights, 5 to 8 words per card, 2 lines max, 1.5 s per line, inner 80% safe area, 36 px floor at 1080 wide, 150 to 250 ms enters. One day. https://www.rocketshiphq.com/text-overlays-video-ads-mobile/
8. **Sound**: licensed track, 5 to 8 sparse SFX, silence before the reveal, two-pass loudnorm to −14 LUFS / −1 dBTP, SRT captions for X and LinkedIn. One day plus license. https://www.nicolastiteux.com/en/blog/sound-design-for-motion-design/ https://www.forasoft.com/learn/audio-for-video/articles-audio/lufs-targets-per-platform-2026
9. **Motion blur**: CameraMotionBlur at 6 to 10 samples, 180 degree shutter, on camera moves only. Hours to wire, sample-count multiplier on render time. https://www.remotion.dev/docs/motion-blur/camera-motion-blur
10. **Glass done properly**: MeshTransmissionMaterial with thickness, attenuation, roughness blur and small dispersion, shared buffer; or a path-traced hero still via three-gpu-pathtracer for the one shot that needs stacked refraction. One to three days. http://drei.docs.pmnd.rs/shaders/mesh-transmission-material https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-gpu-pathtracer

Not closable in this pipeline: area-light shadows, multi-bounce GI, order-independent stacked refraction, true TRAA. Those are the reasons to fork to Blender Cycles (option b) for a hero shot or the whole piece.
